## II. Theorem Statement (Existence Form)

Let $(T_g)_{g\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a recursive tier-sequence such that for each $g$:

```math
\mathcal{O}_R(T_g)\subseteq \mathcal{M}_S,
\qquad
\mathrm{Inv}_{\mathcal{T}_I}(T_g),
\qquad
\text{and}\quad \mathcal{L}_S\ \text{is preserved.}
```

Then the recursive system **admits at least one fixed structure** $T^\ast$ satisfying

```math
\mathcal{O}_R(T^\ast)=T^\ast.
```

Such a $T^\ast$ is called a **Renewal Fixed Point**.

## III. Interpretive Clarification

This theorem does not assert inevitability of convergence from arbitrary initial conditions.

It asserts instead that:

Within the invariant domain defined by ${\mathcal{M}}_S$,  
under consistency governed by ${\mathcal{O}}_R$,  
and conservation enforced by ${\mathcal{L}}_S$,

there exists at least one structurally self-consistent tier-state.

The architecture permits equilibrium.

## IV. Stability as an Admissible Outcome

If the recursive sequence further satisfies

```math
\left\|\mathcal{O}_R(T_{g+1}) - \mathcal{O}_R(T_g)\right\| \to 0
\quad \text{as } g\to\infty,
```

then convergence toward a Renewal Fixed Point becomes dynamically plausible within the invariant manifold.

This condition strengthens admissibility into observable stability.
